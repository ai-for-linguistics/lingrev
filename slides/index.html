Review your paper
with an AI assistant

Give the assistant a prompt. It starts the review and builds a dashboard.

Then ask for checks, rewrites, or a different view.

The assistant does the work. You guide the review and decide what to revise.

Launch prompt: ../PROMPT.md

The kit is an assistant-led workflow. The local commands manage files and render the review. They do not judge a manuscript.
1 / What to give it

A paper is enough to begin

Give it a paper as a PDF or readable source. Add relevant papers and links, or the whole working folder: code, data, figures, and notes.

Identify the current draft and any files to exclude.

Goals, a deadline, or a target venue help when you have them. You can add them later.

Launch prompt: ../PROMPT.md

A working folder can give context, but the assistant should respect exclusions and identify the current manuscript.
2 / The first review

Feedback with evidence

Overview summarizes the review, your goals, and the strengths to preserve.

In Comments, sort the table and expand a row for evidence, checks, and next steps. Compare current and proposed text side by side.

Coverage & sources records what was inspected, checked, or left open. A missing comment does not mean a check passed.

3 / Two interfaces

Talk in the chat.
Read in the dashboard.

Keep them side by side. Ask for a change, then reload the page.

Question a finding, request a check, ask for a rewrite, and even ask for a change in the dashboard itself.

How to illustrates how the work and report can evolve. Specific requests to copy are in Comments; paste one into the chat to continue.

Launch prompt: ../PROMPT.md

The chat is the input and the report is the output. Ask a question in the chat and reload after the assistant updates the report.

Test a claim, compare literature, improve a principle, or check a new draft.

Expand the work with new arguments and tests, alternative rewrites, a slide deck or handout, or changes to the report itself. How to illustrates the possibilities.

Launch prompt: ../PROMPT.md

5 / Before sharing

Use it for your own work

That is the recommended use. Share others’ manuscripts or confidential material only with permission.

Material you share may become accessible to AI agents, providers, and companies operating the tools.

Handling and retention depend on the service and settings. Check them before sharing unpublished or sensitive work.

Launch prompt: ../PROMPT.md

Do not imply that the kit grants confidentiality or controls a provider’s retention policy. The user needs to choose an appropriate service.
6 / Costs

Set a budget

Your assistant uses your credits or tokens. Long reviews and new analyses can cost more.

Ask for an estimate before big steps. Start small, then choose what is worth doing next.

Author revision time, assistant runtime, and token costs are different. Estimates may be uncertain.

Launch prompt: ../PROMPT.md

A short review should exist before optional expensive work begins. Budget estimates are estimates, not measured usage.

The launch prompt

Download the kit, then copy this into your assistant. It handles setup and asks for missing material.

I want to use lingrev to review my work.

1. Find the lingrev kit in the folder, attachment, or address I supplied.
   If I supplied lingrev-beta.zip, unpack it. Otherwise look in the current
   folder and Downloads for lingrev-beta.zip or a lingrev folder containing
   BOOTSTRAP.md. If you cannot find it, ask me where it is.
2. Ask me what to review if I have not already supplied it. I can give you
   just the paper, the paper with relevant literature (PDFs or links), or
   my whole working folder, including code, data, and notes. Identify the
   current manuscript if there are several drafts. Ask where to create the review project
   only if no suitable location is clear. Optional goals can come later.
3. Put a copy of the kit in <project>/utils without overwriting existing
   files. If utils/BOOTSTRAP.md already belongs to lingrev, resume that
   project. Read utils/BOOTSTRAP.md and follow it.
4. Briefly explain sharing with AI providers, token costs, and the need to
   check the feedback, as described in the bootstrap. Then set up the
   workspace and produce a first review automatically. Keep originals
   intact. Save the dashboard before offering optional deeper work.
5. Consider scientific as well as editorial feedback: counterevidence,
   candidate facts, cross-linguistic extensions, clearer or improved
   principles, terminology, local rewrites, paper structure, new arguments,
   experiments, plots and tables, and future work. Check typos, numerical
   errors, and incorrect applications of principles or methods as well.
   Consider formal checks,
   replaying supplied code, or new analyses when useful and feasible. Label
   LLM judgments as tentative and propose real tests. Show current and
   proposed passages side by side.
   Offer alternative abstracts, titles, main headlines, or paper structures
   with possible emphases in bullet points when useful. Suggest additional
   plots or a small online analysis when they could clarify a claim.
6. I will use the chat to iterate and the tabbed dashboard to read the
   review. Use the kit's current report template: Overview, Comments,
   Coverage & sources, and How to. Comments is a sortable table with
   expandable details. My work, the review, and the form of the report can
   change at any time. Start with a concise first pass unless I requested
   a focused or deep review; follow my scope and budget without treating
   the first pass as a limit on later work.
   Keep specific suggestions in Comments. How to should invite me to
   choose comments and expand my study, review, or report, with the setup
   and possibilities pictures side by side. Follow the bootstrap for the layout.
